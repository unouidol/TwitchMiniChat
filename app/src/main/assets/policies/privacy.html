TwitchMiniChat Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-11-13

TwitchMiniChat ("TMC") is an Android Twitch chat client. This Privacy Policy explains what data TMC processes, why it is processed, how long it may be kept, and how users can request deletion or contact the developer.

1. Developer and contact

Developer: Unouidol Interactive
Privacy contact: unouidol.interactive@gmail.com

2. What data TMC may process

  • Twitch account information needed to sign in and use the app, such as Twitch username and related profile identifiers.
  • Authentication and session data needed to connect to Twitch chat.
  • Selected Twitch channels and account preferences stored by the app.
  • Push notification registration data, such as FCM token and app/device registration identifiers. Each registration also carries the phone's manufacturer and model name, as Android reports them.
  • The alert settings and Most Wanted watchlists of each account, sent to the TwitchMiniChat server so that it can send the alerts you choose: which spawn alerts are on, including whether event spawns are, and, when you use Most Wanted, whether it is on and the Pokémon you picked for it. Alert settings are sent when you change them, and again when the app starts until the server has confirmed them. A Most Wanted watchlist is sent only when you save it or switch it on or off.
  • Public Twitch chat content shown inside the app and cached chat history needed for app functionality.
  • Message report data submitted by users, which may include message text, username, channel, message ID, and timestamp.
  • Blocked users lists and other local app settings stored on the device.
  • Operational, security, and abuse-prevention records needed to run the service and process deletion or moderation-related requests.
  • Crash and error diagnostics, when crash reporting is enabled: device model, Android version, app version, and a technical stack trace. These reports are technical only. They do not include your Twitch account, channels, chat content, message reports, or any identifier you chose.
  • A local diagnostic record, kept to investigate problems users report. It records when the app loads chat history and with what result, app and connection events, and how each push alert was delivered and whether its sound played, together with the phone's sound settings at that moment (ringer mode, notification volume, Do Not Disturb, whether audio was going to a Bluetooth device), each with its time and with the Twitch account name and channel it relates to. It never contains chat messages, passwords, or sign-in tokens. The record stays on the device and is never sent automatically, to the developer or to anyone else. It leaves the device only if the user deliberately chooses to share a copy, and a shared copy also names the device model and Android version. Every data reset in Safety & Privacy erases it.

3. Why TMC uses data

  • To authenticate users and connect them to Twitch chat.
  • To display chat history, account settings, and selected channels.
  • To provide optional push alerts and manage device registrations.
  • To support in-app safety tools such as report and block features.
  • To protect service integrity, reduce abuse, investigate misuse, and diagnose technical issues.
  • To detect and fix crashes and malfunctions, including on device models the developer cannot test directly.
  • To respond to data control and deletion requests.
  • To show a channel's stream inside the app when you choose to, and to tell you when a newer version of the app is available.

4. Who data may be shared with

  • Twitch, to provide Twitch authentication and chat-related functionality.
  • The Pokémon Community Game extension, when you choose to open it. It is a Twitch extension operated by a third party, not by TMC. It runs inside the app's built-in browser and communicates with its own operator's servers, under that operator's terms. TMC only reads what the extension shows on your phone, such as your Poké Ball counts and the Pokémon missing from your Pokédex, to offer its helpers; it does not change the extension or what the extension sends. What TMC reads stays on your phone, with one exception: when you tap Update Pokédex, the list of spawnable Pokémon missing from your Pokédex is sent to the TwitchMiniChat server, with your account's identifier and Twitch username, so that the server can send the spawn alerts you choose.
  • Google / Firebase services used for push notifications and, when crash reporting is enabled, for crash and error diagnostics (Firebase Crashlytics).
  • Hosting or infrastructure providers used to operate the app backend.
  • Cloudflare, which publishes the TwitchMiniChat server on the internet. Every request between the app and that server passes through Cloudflare's infrastructure, and so do the sign-in pages your browser opens on that server. Cloudflare therefore handles your IP address, the requests themselves and the server's answers, under Cloudflare's own privacy policy.
  • GitHub, which hosts two things the app loads. When you show a stream, its player page is loaded from GitHub Pages: that request shows GitHub your IP address, the standard identification of the app's built-in browser, and the name of the channel whose stream you open. At most once a day, the app asks GitHub whether a newer version exists: that request shows GitHub your IP address and the app's fixed name, "TwitchMiniChat-Android", and nothing about your phone. Neither request includes your Twitch account, a sign-in token, or anything that identifies your phone or this installation, and the developer receives no record of either. When you tap the update button, your browser downloads the new version from GitHub. GitHub handles these requests under its own privacy statement.
  • Authorities or legal recipients where required by law or legal process.

5. Retention

Data is kept only as long as reasonably needed for app functionality, security, abuse prevention, moderation review, and deletion handling.

  • Some local settings remain on-device until the user clears app data, removes them, or uninstalls the app.
  • Removing one account does not by itself remove everything kept for it: that account’s sign-in with the TwitchMiniChat server is kept until the server confirms that its alerts are off. Erasing this device’s data removes it in any case.
  • The local diagnostic record is limited in size: older entries are discarded as new ones are written. It is erased by every data reset in Safety & Privacy, and by uninstalling the app.
  • Some server-side records may be retained for a limited time for operational integrity, audit, abuse prevention, fraud prevention, or legal compliance.
  • Cached chat history may be retained only for a limited period needed for service functionality.

6. User controls

  • Users can report messages in-app.
  • Users can block users in-app.
  • Users can manage privacy and data controls from the app’s Safety & Privacy section.
  • Users can turn crash reporting off at any time in Safety & Privacy. Once turned off, no further crash or error reports are sent from that device, and any report still waiting to be sent is discarded.
  • Users can request deletion of app-related data. See Data Deletion.

7. Security

TMC uses reasonable technical measures to protect data in transit and at rest where applicable. No method of transmission or storage is perfectly secure.

8. Children

TMC is not directed to children. Users are responsible for complying with Twitch rules, platform requirements, and applicable law.

9. Changes to this policy

This policy may be updated from time to time. Updated versions will replace the previous version inside the app and on the official web policy page.
